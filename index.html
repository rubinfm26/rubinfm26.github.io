Our Purpose

About the Hack-Week

Foundation models are trained once and reused by everyone, and sharing them is the point of this hack-week.

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory alert stream has been public since February 2026, delivering roughly 10 million alerts per night. Foundation models are the most powerful tools modern AI offers for data at this scale, but training one takes curated data, GPU time, and specialist engineering that no single research group can marshal. Over the past two years, teams at the NSF-Simons SkAI Institute built SELDON and StarEmbed, foundation models for multi-band light curves and for variable stars respectively, trained on fully labeled simulations of LSST (ELAsTiCC).

This hack-week shares those models with the broader Rubin community. The SkAI model builders will run SELDON and StarEmbed on live alert-stream sources and publish the result inside Hyrax (the LINCC Frameworks tool for model-agnostic exploration of survey data) as a shared representation, a compact numerical summary of every source that anyone can train small models on without retraining the big one. It is the first time a SkAI foundation model runs at survey scale on real, operationally interrupted LSST observations rather than smooth simulated cadence.

Participants build their own projects on that shared representation (transient classification, photometric redshifts, anomaly search, galaxy mergers, weak lensing, and catalog cross-matching), working in teams alongside the SkAI model builders, the Hyrax engineers, and the broker specialists. The code and tutorials go back to the public Hyrax repository and the Rubin Data Academy.

If you are a graduate student or postdoc in DESC, TVSSC, or ISSC and comfortable with Python and light-curve data, this hack-week is for you (no prior experience with foundation models is needed, and we send a plain-language primer and Rubin Data Academy material in advance). You leave with a working project on real Rubin data, code merged into Hyrax, and the tools to apply to your own science. There is no registration fee, meals during the hack-week are provided, and we have travel and lodging support for out-of-town participants, with priority for early-career researchers from smaller institutions. The hack-week will be held November 4–6, 2026 at the SkAI Institute in Chicago (in the John Hancock Center). It is graciously supported by the LSST Discovery Alliance through the LINCC Frameworks Community Events program.

What We'll Build

Hack-Week Goals

Three days to a working deployment of SkAI foundation models on real Rubin data, inside a framework the whole community can use

01

A Shared Representation of Live Rubin Alerts

The SkAI model builders run SELDON and StarEmbed on live alert-stream sources and publish the shared representation inside Hyrax as a resource the whole Rubin community can use.

02

Your Own Science on Top of It

Train lightweight classifiers, photometric-redshift estimators, and anomaly searches on the shared representation. Real labels at Rubin's depth are scarce, and small probes on a fixed representation need far fewer of them.

03

Simulation-to-Real Validation

Measure how models trained on simulated LSST data (ELAsTiCC) perform against Rubin's real operating cadence, with Data Preview 2 (real imaging released in July 2026) as a static cross-check.

04

Catalog Cross-Matching at Scale

Pull archival photometry and Dark Energy Survey postage stamps for the sources flagged in the shared representation, using the SCiMMA cross-match service (built on LSDB).

05

Code, Tutorials, and Merged Pull Requests

Contribute working code back to the public Hyrax repository and write tutorial notebooks for the Rubin Data Academy.

06

Community Paper

Outline a community paper on what the shared representation does and does not capture in the real alert stream, with every participant team as a contributor.
